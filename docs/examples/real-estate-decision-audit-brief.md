# Real Estate Decision Audit Example Brief

This is a paste-ready example brief for demo use, not a first-class vertical
template, not a first-class backend runtime template, and not a runtime pack. It
is for a local operator workflow. It is not investment advice, not financial
advice, not legal advice, not tax advice, not appraisal or valuation
certification, not lending or credit underwriting, not public SaaS, not a
guaranteed buy/sell/hold recommendation engine, and not a new reasoning mode.

Human review is required before any output is shared or acted on. Real estate
findings are directional, not guarantees.

## Real Estate Decision Question

Should Harborline Properties hold and renovate the 48-unit Eastbank Apartments
asset, sell it after stabilizing occupancy, or pause major capex until market
and financing assumptions are validated?

## Decision Type

- Decision type: `hold/renovate/sell`.
- Decision to support: decide whether to approve a 90-day diligence and capex
  planning sprint, defer the renovation plan, or prepare a sale process.
- Alternatives being compared: hold with phased renovation, stabilize then
  sell, or defer major capex for one quarter.
- Known non-goals: no certified valuation, no tax structuring, no lending or
  credit decision, and no autonomous external action.

## Property Or Portfolio Context

Eastbank Apartments is a 48-unit garden-style multifamily asset in a secondary
market. Current occupancy is 88 percent after several deferred-maintenance
issues. The sponsor has owned the asset for six years and is deciding whether
to invest in unit renovations and common-area repairs before refinancing or
selling. The property manager reports leasing demand is stable, but resident
turnover has increased.

## Market And Submarket Assumptions

- Local employment is steady, but two competing renovated assets are offering
  concessions.
- Broker notes suggest renovated units may command a moderate premium, but the
  comps are based on larger properties with better amenities.
- New supply is limited in the immediate submarket, but older properties are
  increasing concessions.
- The weakest assumption is whether renovated-unit rent premiums are durable
  enough to justify capex and disruption.

## Rent/Revenue Assumptions

- Current average rent is based on the rent roll supplied by the property
  manager.
- Renovated-unit premium is estimated at `$120-$175` per month, based on broker
  notes and two nearby comps.
- Vacancy during renovation is expected to increase for two to three months.
- Collections have been stable, but turnover could reduce near-term revenue.
- Revenue assumptions are planning inputs and must be reviewed by the operator
  and real-estate experts.

## Expense And Capex Assumptions

- Operating expenses are based on a T12 summary and a draft 2026 budget.
- The initial renovation budget is `$18,000-$24,000` per unit for 20 units, plus
  common-area repairs.
- Insurance and property tax increases are not fully modeled.
- Deferred maintenance could increase contingency needs.
- Contractor pricing has not been independently validated.

## Financing Assumptions

- Current debt matures in 18 months.
- A refinance may be possible if occupancy and NOI improve, but proceeds and
  covenant assumptions are not confirmed.
- Interest-rate sensitivity could materially change the hold decision.
- Lender and credit review are required before relying on financing
  assumptions.

## Sensitivity/Scenario Assumptions

- Base case: phased renovation, moderate rent premium, stable occupancy after
  disruption.
- Downside case: lower rent premium, higher vacancy, capex overrun, and tighter
  refinance proceeds.
- Upside case: faster lease-up, stronger renewal demand, and lower contingency
  use.
- Key sensitivity: whether rent premium and occupancy recovery offset capex and
  financing risk.
- Evidence gap: current comps and contractor bids are not strong enough for a
  final decision.

## Operational Constraints

The property manager can support only eight to ten unit turns per quarter
without outside help. Resident disruption, contractor availability, leasing
capacity, and delayed materials could affect execution. The sponsor also needs
to preserve enough liquidity for emergency repairs and lender reserves.

## Regulatory/Legal/Tax Questions For Human Experts

The operator should route lease, title, insurance, property tax, contractor,
permitting, fair housing, lender covenant, and local regulatory questions to
the right human experts. Any sale, refinance, or renovation approval should be
reviewed by legal, tax, finance, lender, credit, insurance, and local market
experts before external action.

## Risk Classification And Human Oversight

Suggested classification: `minimal_risk` for internal analysis, subject to
operator review. The rationale is that the package supports an internal
decision review and does not automate tenant, lending, tax, legal, valuation,
or external transaction decisions. Human oversight must include sponsor review,
asset-management review, property-management review, finance review, and expert
review of legal, tax, valuation, appraisal, lending, credit, insurance,
engineering, environmental, or regulatory claims.

## First 30/60/90-Day Diligence Plan

- First 30 days: refresh rent comps, validate the rent roll and T12, confirm
  occupancy drivers, obtain contractor bids, identify urgent maintenance, and
  assign reviewers.
- First 60 days: stress-test rent premium, vacancy, capex, interest rate, and
  refinance scenarios; review lease and permitting questions; update the capex
  plan.
- First 90 days: prepare a reviewed hold/renovate/sell decision package with
  monitoring owners, warning thresholds, and stop conditions.

## Success Metrics And Monitoring

- Primary metrics: occupancy, renewal rate, renovated-unit premium, NOI, capex
  variance, leasing velocity, resident disruption, and refinance readiness.
- Baseline: current occupancy is 88 percent; rent and expense baselines should
  be verified against source files.
- Monitoring cadence: weekly during active diligence and monthly after any
  approved renovation plan.
- Warning threshold: contractor bids exceed budget by more than 15 percent,
  occupancy drops below 85 percent, or rent comps weaken materially.
- Stop threshold: unresolved legal, tax, lending, credit, safety, environmental,
  or permitting issue; material capex overrun; or evidence that rent premiums do
  not support the plan.

## Known Evidence

- Current rent roll and occupancy summary from the property manager.
- Draft T12 summary and 2026 budget.
- Broker notes with two renovated-unit comps.
- Preliminary renovation scope from asset management.
- Debt maturity schedule and high-level refinance concern.
- Property manager feedback on resident turnover and leasing capacity.

## Unknowns

- Whether renovated-unit rent premiums are durable.
- Whether contractor bids and contingency are realistic.
- Whether insurance, tax, and repair cost increases are adequately modeled.
- Whether refinance proceeds are sufficient after rate and covenant review.
- Whether resident disruption could weaken occupancy during renovation.

## What A Good Recommendation Should Resolve

- Whether to hold and renovate, stabilize then sell, or defer major capex.
- Which assumptions need expert review before approval.
- Which diligence actions should happen in the first 30/60/90 days.
- Which market, rent, expense, capex, financing, legal, tax, operational, and
  expert-review gaps are most material.
- What monitoring signals define success, warning, and stop conditions.
- What evidence would change the directional finding.

## Suggested Files / Evidence To Upload If Available

- Property summary or sponsor decision memo.
- Rent roll, occupancy report, renewal report, and lease abstracts.
- T12/T3, budget, expense details, property tax bill, and insurance quote.
- Renovation scope, capex budget, contractor bids, inspection notes, and
  engineering or environmental reports.
- Market report, submarket report, rent comps, sales comps, broker opinion, and
  supply pipeline notes.
- Financing term sheet, debt schedule, lender notes, and covenant summary.
- Legal, tax, appraisal, valuation, insurance, permitting, zoning, or
  regulatory review notes.

## Expected Output Types

- Client-safe after review: `report`, `client_dossier`,
  `client_monitoring_template`.
- Operator-only: `operator_dossier`, `operator_monitoring_template`.
- Internal archive: `machine_archive`.

## Human Review Reminder

Treat hypotheses, scores, real estate findings, thresholds, and recommendations
as review material. The operator must verify evidence maturity, validate market,
rent/revenue, expense, capex, financing, sensitivity/scenario, operational, and
diligence assumptions, route investment, financial, legal, tax, appraisal,
valuation, lending, credit, environmental, engineering, insurance, regulatory,
and local market claims to the right human experts, resolve critical review
questions, confirm human oversight, and decide what is safe to share.
