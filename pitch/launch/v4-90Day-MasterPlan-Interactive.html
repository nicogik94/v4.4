AI output quality control protocol

[ARVV FIX #2 — Critical gap addressed] This was entirely missing from the original plan. The ARVV premortem identified "AI hallucination causing bad first-client experience" as the #1 failure mode (RPN=225). This QC protocol is now mandatory for every client deliverable.
Rule: NEVER send raw engine output to a client
Every deliverable goes through this 5-step check. Budget 2 hours. Non-negotiable.
1
Read every findingDoes each one make logical sense? Would you stake your reputation on it? If anything feels generic or questionable, rewrite it manually or remove it.
2
Verify all numbersEvery statistic, percentage, or dollar figure the AI generates must be independently verified. Google every number. AI hallucinates citations at 28–88% depending on domain (Stanford).
3
Run the opposite testFor each recommendation, state the exact opposite. If the opposite is obviously stupid ("Don't improve your dashboard"), the original might be a platitude. Real insights have reasonable opposites.
4
Run SQI on your own outputUse the SQI agent to critique the strategy. If SQI scores below 70, revise before delivery. Pay attention to the "weakest link" dimension.
5
Executive summary testRead only the executive summary out loud. Does a non-expert understand the recommendation and WHY it's the right call? If not, rewrite in plain language.
Why this matters

Two hours of QC on a $2,500 deliverable = $1,250/hr effective rate insurance. One hallucinated recommendation can destroy your reputation before it's built. The first 3 clients determine whether you have a business or a hobby.

Outreach templates + objection handling

LinkedIn DM templates
Network activation (week 1 — warm contacts)
"Hey [Name], I've launched an AI consulting practice focused on helping companies make better strategic decisions. I've built a system that analyzes business challenges through 30 analytical frameworks simultaneously. If you know anyone struggling with a strategic decision — market entry, resource allocation, competitive response — I'd love to help. Happy to do a free initial analysis to demonstrate the approach."
Cold outreach — timeline hook (highest reply rate: 10.67%)
"Hi [Name], I noticed [Company] is evaluating [AI/automation/strategic decision]. I recently helped a similar [industry] company cut their decision-analysis time from 3 weeks to 3 days using multi-framework AI analysis. Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation about how we did it?"
Cold email — timeline hook
Subject: How [similar company] cut decision time by 85%

Hi [Name],

[Company] is [expanding/launching/evaluating] — that usually means dozens of strategic decisions in a short window.

I built an AI system that runs 30 analytical frameworks simultaneously on any business decision. A [industry] company used it last month to evaluate a market entry in 3 days instead of 3 weeks.

Worth a 15-minute call this week?
Follow-up (day 3 — 50–70% of replies come from follow-ups)
[ARVV FIX #9] Removed LinkedIn's marketing claim of "300% higher InMail response rate." Independent data shows InMail performs significantly better than cold email but the 3x figure is unverified.
"Quick follow-up on my note below — thought this might be relevant given [recent company news/hiring/product launch]. Happy to share the framework if it'd be useful."
Objection handling
"We don't have the budget."
"I understand. If this analysis saves you [X] in avoided mistakes or accelerated decisions, it pays for itself in [timeframe]. Would a smaller pilot scope at $1,500 make sense as a starting point?"
"We've never worked with a solo consultant."
"That's exactly why you get better results — direct access to the expert, zero overhead markup, and an AI system that analyzes from 30 perspectives simultaneously. No junior associate doing the work."
"How do we know this works?"
"I structure engagements with clear milestones so you see value before the project is half over. And the pilot rate means your risk is $1,500 — less than one day of a Big Four consultant."
"We need to think about it."
"Of course. What specifically would you want to think through? I want to make sure I've addressed everything." (Surface the real objection.)
"Can you just send a proposal?"
"Absolutely. Before I do, let me make sure I understand your situation so the proposal is relevant. Can we do a quick 15-minute call this week?" (Never send without a discovery call.)
